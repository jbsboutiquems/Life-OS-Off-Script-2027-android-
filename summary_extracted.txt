T H E  M I S S I O N
Not a productivity system. A mirror.
Life OS exists for people who are done performing productivity. It does not ask anyone to optimize,
hustle, or keep up. It asks them to notice their own life, write it down honestly, and change it on
purpose. The one-liner that holds the whole brand together: Oﬀ*Script is a life design brand that
helps people stop living by default and start living diﬀerently, intentionally, and on purpose.
The philosophy, in ﬁve lines
Chaos is not disorder. A messy season is material, not failure.
Monotony equals death. The slogan on the product is Boredom = Death - boredom is for the
average person; this brand is not for average.
Values before performance. What matters to you outranks how impressive it looks.
Identity evolves. You are allowed to become someone else, more than once.
Learn who you are, one year at a time. Growth is annual, seasonal, and patient - organized
around one word a year and no more than six core focal goals.
Who made it, and why it sounds like this
Life OS was created by Amber Marie Wiggins of Kosciusko, Mississippi - entrepreneur, designer of
personal organization systems, and author of a book in progress. Her named creative inspiration is
Benrik (Ben Carey and Henrik Delehag), the London duo behind the absurd daily-task diaries that sold
more than 400,000 copies. Her standing creative rule follows from that lineage: start outrageous and
loud, then tone down - because starting moderate and trying to get louder later never works. The
voice stays irreverent and anti-performative on purpose; anything that sounds like optimization
theater gets cut.
The brand system
Colors are navy, cream, teal, hot pink, and gold, with a compass-and-star motif and the styled name
Oﬀ*Script. Editions are color-coded by year: 2027 is the classic teal-and-pink cosmic nebula, 2028 is
aurora-and-cosmic zodiac styling, and 2029 is gold nebula. The website's pink-teal theme (teal
#2da2ee, hot pink #ea4798 on cosmic dark) is the reference the ﬂagship PDF and the app match.

The 2027 Chaos Year ﬂagship
The ﬂagship is a 1,252-page dated planner (the current master is version 5) built around 365 daily
spreads, weekly debriefs, and monthly and quarterly reviews, with Oﬀ*Script asterisks and Boredom =
Death headers throughout and the Life OS footer on every page. A proofread by CreaO caught real
print defects - a text overlap on the January 4 spread, stray white boxes over header ﬁelds, and an
obsolete website URL still sitting on twelve monthly dividers - and those ﬁxes are queued for a version
6 before anything goes to print. That is the process working as intended: the book gets read hard
before a buyer ever sees it.
The physical Chaos Year Kit is two hardcover spiral volumes (about 612 pages each), sticker sheets, a
pen or marker, a snap-in bookmark, a magnet, a bumper sticker, app access, and the standard digital
PDF. Pricing waits on the printer's quote on purpose - the rule is a thin margin is ﬁne, but the buyer
must feel they got their money's worth.

The wedding line, and Life OS in your pocket
Becoming an Oﬀ*Script Bride in 365 Days
The wedding planner is a 511-page book (renamed September 26, 2026) that passed full quality
review, with a Dress Studio section at pages 492-502. Provisional pricing is $20 pre-order, $25 after the
drop, and $40 printed. The twelve zodiac editions run 1,289 pages - beyond print-on-demand limits,
so they are PDF-only unless split into volumes.
The companion wedding app is deliberately free and link-based, with no license wall: party members
open their own HQ page by link, one page per role across all seven wedding-party roles, with roles the
couple can add, delete, or restore. The old bride-to-maid-of-honor chat wall was replaced with
“magic” post-it notes on the HQ page - no direct messages, no backend, nothing to moderate. Paid
value lives in the print and PDF; the app is the funnel.
The Life OS app
The app is a web app at heart - React and TypeScript with a Node backend - wrapped in a native
Android shell with Capacitor, so it installs as a sideloadable APK, runs in any browser, and adds to a
phone's home screen. An Android debug build (app ID app.oﬀscript.lifeos) was veriﬁed October 2,
2026; iOS is scaﬀolded and needs a Mac to build; the desktop experience is the browser and
progressive web app.
Daily life, structured: daily entries with a real 365-day holiday mapping, weekly debriefs, and
snapshots Mei can read back to you.
Pinned days that are ours: February 9 is Mardi Gras, April 20 is High Spirits Day, and September
27 - the founder's birthday - is Founder's Day, in hot pink and neon cyan. The app's daily view
mirrors the ﬂagship's special spreads.
Sign-on, your choice: Google, Apple, Facebook, email, and SMS were all approved; Google,
Facebook, and email are free, while Apple and SMS stay dark until spending is approved.
Hardened, not hopeful: admin authentication on the hidden wall, user blocking, and passing lint,
build, and tests as of the September 26, 2026 hardening pass.
Honest status: the Android repository has diverged from GitHub and will not be auto-merged;
deployment to Railway waits on the founder's account; and the apps stay local until she says
otherwise. Open items are listed, not hidden - that is the brand.

T H E  N E T W O R K  ·  M E I
A mailbox, not a timeline
The Oﬀ*Script network: private by design
The community layer - the Chaos Wall and private direct messages - will not be openly available. It is
gated and private, like the app itself. The rule for social is outbound-only: you message your people
directly. There are no feeds, no scrolling, and no algorithm deciding what you see. Conversations ride
the Life OS account, so the same people and the same threads are there whether you open the app,
the phone, or the book device. Inside the app, the Playdate game corner keeps the same spirit - no
timers, no scores, nothing to lose at.
Chat is also where the device's pen earns its keep a second way: handwriting recognition turns scrawl
into a sent text message, the way Apple Scribble and Samsung's pen-to-text already work. Write “on
my way” in the reply box and it sends as typed text. Anything longer gets the small on-screen
keyboard - but direct messages are short by nature, so the pen covers almost all of it.
Mei, the AI companion
Mei is the founder's “User Deﬁned” Messages Improved bot, and every AI endpoint in the app runs
through Mei, called directly by bot ID - no keys, no tokens - a setup tested live on September 23,
2026. Ask Mei pulls your last seven days of entries, recent snapshots, and your latest weekly debrief
into the conversation, token-bounded, so Mei answers from your actual week instead of generic
advice. The same material feeds diagnostics.
Two exceptions were approved on October 2, 2026, and only two: Gemini powers the AI media studio
(music, image, and video generation) and a pre-existing fallback inside the diagnostics endpoint.
Everything else stays on Mei, and the Gemini key lives server-side only - never in the repository, never
in the client.
Why this matters: an AI that reads your journal is a trust decision, not a feature. One bot, one
ID, no scattered keys - the smallest possible surface for the most personal data in the product.

T H E  B O O K  D E V I C E  ·  I N C L U S I V E  B Y  D E S I G N
The crease is the spine
The hardware vision is a foldable book: one screen that opens like a paper book. The insight that
unlocked it - in the founder's words, the crease is the spine. Nobody writes across the gutter of a
paper book, so nobody drags a pen across the fold; the crease lands exactly where a book divides, and
the interface simply agrees with it. It is the Nintendo DS idea at ten times the size: beloved, not weird -
Nintendo sold 150 million of them.
Each screen has a job: left does A, right does B - planning left, journaling right. No window
management, ever.
Two postures: a 360-degree friction hinge gives ﬂat book mode (primary - eyes and pen on the
same page, like paper) and propped laptop mode, where the top screen rests or goes passive while
you journal below.
Built like an appliance: weight, battery, and brains on the right side for balance, a single USB-C
port, locked bootloader with veriﬁed boot, and the Life OS interface as the whole experience -
AOSP plumbing underneath, never exposed.
Wi-Fi only, oﬄine-ﬁrst: everything works with no connection and syncs at home. GPS needs no
cell signal, so geocache-style missions work in the woods with map packs downloaded ahead. “It
cannot interrupt you from the cloud because it is not in the cloud” is a selling point.
Not for 2027: the horizon after the paper planner and the app. Prototyping starts on existing
hardware - a Lenovo ThinkPad X1 Fold (16-inch) or ASUS Zenbook 17 Fold is the dev kit today.
Nobody's hands are wrong
Inclusion is a speciﬁcation here, not a paragraph of good intentions. The input trio covers every hand -
and no hands:
INPUT HOW IT WORKS
Pen-ﬁrst Haptic stylus on a textured, paper-feel screen; handwriting-to-text keeps the
journal searchable without typing. Rollable screens were considered and rejected -
a scroll is not a book.
Keyboard-only mode A full setting: everything reachable from the on-screen keyboard, no stylus
required. The popup keyboard otherwise appears only for passwords, search, and
URLs.
Voice Full voice control and dictation - the whole device operable hands-free. Dictating a
Chaos Wall post while pacing the kitchen may be the most Oﬀ*Script thing it does.
Signing (roadmap) Fingerspelling-to-text (26 letters plus numbers, camera-based) is the tractable ﬁrst
step. Full sign language recognition is a research problem industry-wide and is
claimed as a horizon goal, not a shipped promise. All camera processing runs on-
device - a camera that watches your hands never phones home.
